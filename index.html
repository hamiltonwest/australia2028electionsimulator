Economy and the world

Demographic swings

Each slider widens or narrows a divide between seats. Seats above average on that measure move one way and seats below average move the other, so the national vote barely changes. Party movements follow each party's 2025 pattern.

Custom rules (advanced)

Each rule moves one party's vote in seats that score higher (or lower) than average on a single Census measure: points per standard deviation from the average seat.

Preferences

One Nation and Coalition voters swap preferences more tightly in regional seats; Greens voters favour Labor more strongly in the inner cities.

Uncertainty
Senate

A double dissolution puts every senator up for election: 12 per state, with a quota of 7.7% instead of 14.3%, which is much easier for minor parties.

Points added to a party's Senate vote only, on top of its House swing. Senate voters back minor parties and personal brands more than House voters do.

    About this page

    An independent election simulator built from a personal Excel model. It is not affiliated with any political party, pollster, broadcaster or the Australian Electoral Commission. XYZ News and its reporters are fictional, and the election-night articles are simulated text: they contain no real quotes.

    Data sources

    • Australian Electoral Commission: 2025 House and Senate results, two-candidate-preferred flows by party, electoral boundaries (March 2025).
    • Published national opinion polls to November 2026, and the DemosAU seat-by-seat MRP (August–September 2026).
    • Australian Bureau of Statistics 2021 Census and the ABS Australian coastline; ATO HELP-debt figures by electorate.
    • Party leaders, dates and changes since 2025: Wikipedia and news reports, as cited in the build notes.

    Photo credits

    Leader photographs and party logos were supplied by the site owner. Their sources and licences must be confirmed, and a credit added for each, before this page is published. Party logos are the property of their parties.
